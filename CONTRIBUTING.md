# Contributing

Outside contributions need a signed contributor license agreement before merge. You retain
ownership of your contribution. The agreement grants StateEngine LLC copyright and patent
rights, including the right to offer contributions under additional commercial or proprietary
licenses; it also requires the submission-date license to remain available for those
contributions.

Read the [individual agreement](docs/cla/individual.md), or the [entity
agreement](docs/cla/entity.md) if an employer or other legal entity owns the contribution. The
governing law is Florida, United States. Only sign if you have authority to grant the rights. An
employee needs employer approval or an authorized entity signature. A parent or guardian must
sign for a minor; contact a maintainer on the pull request to arrange the authorized signature.

## Sign on your pull request

The agreement check gives an exact statement containing the agreement's SHA-256 digest. Post
that statement as a new comment from your own GitHub account. A quoted statement, edited
agreement, or another person's comment does not sign for you. Each outside pull-request author
and primary commit author must sign; use GitHub-verified commit email addresses so the check can
associate commits with accounts. An entity signer must name the legal entity and confirm
authority to sign for it.

The comment is a public electronic signature; do not publish an address, phone number or other
private contact details. The check binds the signature to the exact agreement text and
contributor account, and reruns when comments or pull-request commits change. The repository's
configured internal App and accounts GitHub currently identifies as repository owners,
organization members or collaborators are internal contributors. Each opener and commit author
is checked separately; every other contributor needs a signature. A changed agreement needs a
new signature. Signatures on other pull requests are not carried over automatically.

## Third-party contributions

Do not sign for rights you do not own or control. Clearly identify third-party files and their
licenses in the pull request, retain required notices, and ask a maintainer to review their
inclusion. A third-party license is not replaced by the contributor agreement. Do not submit
material whose license does not permit its proposed use.
